**Written 2026-08-20, in plain language.** Nothing starts until you lock it.

This replaces the nine-stage programme. Same work, corrected sizes, and two things put back
that the earlier merge dropped.

---

## How to read this

Every stage below is **one complete thing a person can do**, start to finish, that they cannot
do today. Not a list of parts — a flow you can walk on a phone.

For each stage you get four lines and nothing else:

- **What you'll be able to do** — in your language, not mine
- **Which apps** — brand, creator, follower
- **Where the work lands** — *Database · Backend · App*. One line. No detail.
- **Size** — Small, Medium or Large, relative to each other

## The mockup rule

**If a stage touches a screen, you see that screen drawn before anything is built — and you
lock it. That lock is the gate. Nothing on a phone gets written before it.**

**The mockups must come out of the ones we already agreed, not out of my head.** We have twelve
agreed boards — the 160-action list, FINAL 1 to 4, the tab arrangement, the creator's
conversations, the shoot, the step history, the navigation spine. A stage's mockup is an
**extract** of those: same layout, same components, same words on the buttons. Not a new design.

Three rules that follow from that:

1. **Where the agreed board and the live app disagree, I show you both and say so** — I never
   quietly pick one. Reading all twelve boards this week I found four places where they
   contradict each other and three where they are simply out of date. Those get surfaced, not
   resolved in silence.
2. **If a screen needs a button the 160-action list doesn't have, the list is wrong and gets
   fixed first.** That is what stops the two apps drifting apart again.
3. **Where nothing was ever drawn, it gets drawn in the same visual language** and locked the
   same way — not built from a paragraph.

**Why this order.** It is the real order the business happens in. A brand meets a creator, they
agree, a campaign is built, the work gets done, the content goes live, followers see it and
buy, they talk about it, and finally everyone sees the numbers.

That is also the **testing** order — each stage produces the real data the next stage needs.
By the time you reach the numbers, there are real numbers.

# Stage 0 · Clear the decks

**What you'll be able to do:** nothing new on a screen. What you get instead is that **every
stage from here on can be tested the day it is built**, instead of showing you empty screens.

Three jobs.

**One — finish the foundation.** Two numbers that almost every screen in this programme leans
on were built in the first phase and then never connected to anything: *"which of the six steps
is this collaboration on"*, and *"how many things need you"*. Four later stages read them. Until
they are connected, those stages either stall, or each app works the number out for itself —
and then your phone and the creator's phone slowly start disagreeing about the same
collaboration. That is the exact problem the foundation was built to prevent.

**Two — build a world to test in.** Right now the live system holds one contract, one
half-finished shoot, one post, and **no sales attributed to any creator at all**. You test by
using the apps. There is nothing to use. So we build a realistic practice world: brands,
creators, agreements, campaigns at different stages, shoots, posts, followers, and real sales
that trace back to a creator and a post.

**Three — tidy up.** Kill the abandoned shoppable-reels work (it turned out to be nothing —
a plan on paper and no code, so removing it costs nothing). Correct four records that say
"not released" when the work is live. And run the phone check that has been owed since the last
follower update.

**Which apps:** none. Nothing visible changes.
**Where the work lands:** Backend · Database (practice data only)
**Size:** Medium

**How you'll know it worked:** I hand you both phones and you find a collaboration sitting at
every one of the six steps, a campaign running, a shoot mid-flight, posts live, and a creator
whose earnings screen shows real money. Today all of those are empty.

# Stage 1 · A brand and a creator find each other and agree

**What you'll be able to do:** search for fashion creators in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, see
how many are in each city before you pick, save fifteen of them as a group, ask them all in one
move, and get an honest answer about how many were actually reached and why the rest weren't.

Each creator sees the request on **the first screen of her Collabs tab** with a badge — not
buried three taps under Profile, which is where Sagar Joshi's request has been sitting unseen
since 15 August.

Both sides can counter, and **every offer that was ever on the table stays visible to both** —
today only the last one survives.

Her Collabs tab becomes six stages: **Invitations · Pitches · To sign · Shoot · Content · Done.**
The brand's app swaps its Alerts tab for a **Creators** tab holding everything that happens
before a campaign exists.

**One outward-facing change in this stage.** You decided on 15 August that approved brands
should be open to creator pitches by default. Right now **1 brand out of 28** accepts pitches,
so a creator looking for someone to pitch sees a single restaurant. Turning this on makes 27
brands reachable — and some of them will find out when a pitch notification arrives. It is
reversible, per brand or all at once, and every brand already has the switch to turn it back
off. **I will not do this without your explicit go on the day.**

**Which apps:** brand · creator
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend · Database (one small change)
**Size:** Large — this is two of the original phases in one flow

# Stage 2 · A brand builds a campaign from the phone

**What you'll be able to do:** take three creators you have already agreed terms with and build
a live campaign without opening a laptop.

Five steps become four. *"Who posts?"* disappears — that was settled when you shook hands.
*"Pick influencers"* becomes *"pick from the agreements you already hold"*. And setting terms
splits in two: **what the creator gets** is simply read back to you because it is already
agreed, and the only genuinely open decision left is **what her followers get** — the offer.

For a selling campaign the question inverts: we stop asking how many posts she must make, and
start asking what channels she brings — because she only earns if she sells.

After this, **one job is left on the desk: adding and pricing products.**

**Which apps:** brand
**Where the work lands:** App · Backend
**Size:** Large

# Stage 3 · Both sides watch the work happen

**What you'll be able to do:** tap a campaign, see its sets; tap a set, see its creators; tap a
creator, see her six steps; tap a step, do the thing. Work owed adds up at every level, and
**every badge equals exactly what is underneath it** — tap a 6 and you can walk it down to a
button, every time.

The creator sees the same six steps with the same numbers. When you ring her and say "we're
stuck on step 3", you are both looking at the same thing.

**This stage gets a real phone test before it ships.** It adds new screens to both apps'
navigation — the kind of change that passes every automatic check and then crashes on a real
device. That check has been skipped six times running. It stops here.

**Which apps:** brand · creator
**Where the work lands:** App (both)
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 4 · The shoot

**What you'll be able to do:** settle a time, send the goods, and confirm what arrived — without
leaving the app. Today the shoot is a paragraph buried inside the contract page with no screen
of its own, so a notification has nowhere to point.

Working style becomes two straight questions instead of three confusing options: **where does
she shoot**, and **are you sending products**.

New: what is actually in the parcel. You list the three sarees. She confirms them one at a time
and flags anything missing. **A shortfall never becomes her problem** — she shoots what arrived,
and you decide whether to resend or drop it.

**Which apps:** brand · creator
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend · Database
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 5 · The content goes live, in the right order

**What you'll be able to do:** know that traffic never lands on an empty shelf. **She cannot
publish to Instagram until at least one shop post is live** — you decided one is enough, not
all of them. Both reviews still run at the same time, so she is never queued twice.

Rejections stop being a free-text box and become a fixed list of technical faults, so "we check
quality, never your concept" becomes a rule the system actually enforces — and faults become
countable.

You get told when a creator publishes a post using **only your products**. A post mixing two
brands tells nobody, because telling one brand reveals the other.

And she can finally open one of her own posts — today she can only see them in a grid.

**Which apps:** creator · brand · your admin desk
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 6 · A follower watches a post and buys

**What you'll be able to do:** swipe up and the next post plays. Videos open full screen; photos
don't enlarge, exactly like Instagram. The creator's face and name sit on top. "Shop this post"
at the bottom.

On a post featuring one brand only, a second button — **Pay at store** — for brands that
actually have a shop.

**One thing to decide here.** That switch already exists and is currently **on for all 28
brands**, and the follower app already shows the button. Nothing has ever asked a brand whether
they have a counter. So the choice is: leave it on and ask brands to correct it, or switch it
off for everyone until each brand answers. *My recommendation: ask first, switch nobody off
without warning.*

**Which apps:** follower · brand
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend · Database
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 7 · One link per channel, one code per post

**What you'll be able to do:** the creator generates one link per platform — Instagram, YouTube,
Facebook, WhatsApp, X, LinkedIn — for her store and for each post. A click on the Instagram
link counts as Instagram. **Every click is ours and exact**, so we never depend on what
Instagram chooses to tell us.

Each post also gets a short code like `A7K2F9`. **The code is the link** — `inflx.in/p/A7K2F9`
opens it from outside, and typing `A7K2F9` in the app finds it. This exists because Instagram
captions cannot carry a clickable link, so the code is a link made of nothing but text.

The channel follows the money for 30 days, matching the commission window. So:
*"Instagram: 340 clicks → 12 orders → ₹18,600."*

**Which apps:** creator · follower
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend · Database
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 9 · A creator who is also a brand

**What you'll be able to do:** one question at signup — "Influencer" or "Brand + Influencer".
He proves he owns the brand by **signing in with that brand's own credentials** — no list to
pick from, no approval from you, any number of brands, at any time.

His own store's products he posts directly and without limit, no campaign needed. Other brands'
products still need a campaign — no shortcut. **He earns no commission on his own sales**,
because it is his own shop — but he still sees exactly what each post sold.

A post can mix his products and a partner brand's. Commission fires per product: the partner's
item pays, his own doesn't.

**Which apps:** creator · follower
**Where the work lands:** App (both) · Backend · Database
**Size:** Medium

# Stage 10 · The numbers, built once

**What you'll be able to do:** one calculation, three views, so nobody ever sees two different
totals for the same sale.

- **The creator** sees everything about her own posts — our numbers exactly, and social numbers
  separately, always stamped with when they were last refreshed.
- **The brand** sees only posts made for it. On a post mixing two brands, each sees only its own
  products' clicks and orders — and **never** the post's likes and comments, because likes
  cannot be split between brands.
- **You** see everything, plus the ability to spot numbers that were invented.

Plus your cut four ways — overall, by creator, by product, by post — and the channel split from
Stage 7.

**Also here:** something finally computes whether a collaboration is *done*. And a post going
dark later never makes her non-compliant — it counted when it was approved.

**Which apps:** all three
**Where the work lands:** App (all three) · Backend
**Size:** Large

# Stage 11 · A feed that learns

**What you'll be able to do:** the follower's feed stops being "newest first" and starts being
"what she'd want". Four dials — what she does, how recent, how near, how well it performs —
**and you can change every weight from admin without an app update.**

Rules that aren't dials: never the same creator twice in a row, never repeat what she's already
seen, drop sold-out, and **15% of slots held for brand-new posts** — otherwise a new post can
never earn a score and dies unseen.

**Last, deliberately.** It needs weeks of real behaviour before its dials mean anything.
Today the system has recorded **two** follower actions in total. Stage 6 onward is what starts
that clock.

**Which apps:** follower
**Where the work lands:** App · Backend
**Size:** Medium

# Deliberately left out

| What | Why |
|---|---|

| **Goods travelling creator to creator** | Four commercial questions with money attached: who pays the courier, who is liable if it's lost, how long creator A has to send it on, and what happens if someone drops out holding the goods. Those are your decisions, not design. |
| **Adding and pricing products on a phone** | Bulk work against a catalogue. A phone version would produce listings that need fixing at a desk anyway. |

# Two things I have decided rather than ask

**The practice world.** I recommended seeding one. It is in Stage 0. If you would rather we
create everything by hand as we go, say so and Stage 0 halves — but then a broken step blocks
every stage behind it and you cannot re-test a stage without walking it again.

**The follower rulebook.** The brand and creator halves are governed by a list of 160 actions —
nothing gets built that isn't on it. The follower half has no such list, and five stages are
follower work. Rather than write another long list now that goes stale, **each follower stage
writes its own list as part of step 1** — its reality check. Same protection, no big document
nobody re-reads.
