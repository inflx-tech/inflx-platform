# The collaboration contract — draft

**Status: DRAFT for the founder's review. Not legal advice, and not reviewed by a lawyer.**
Written 2026-08-28 at his request: *"give me draft of contract how it should be... add all
required details as well as one more detail that is content produced by creator can be used by
inflx platform and related brand in their social media accounts but no exclusive rights on
content but just usage only."*

---

## Part 0 — What this document is for

Two audiences read the same contract and want different things from it.

**She** wants to know: what am I doing, by when, what am I paid, and what happens to my work.
**They** want to know: what am I getting, for how much, and what may I do with it.

So it is written in the order those questions get asked, in plain English, and every number in
it comes from something already agreed rather than being introduced here.

⚠️ **A contract must state nothing new.** Everything below was settled in the pre-campaign
agreement or in the campaign the brand built. If a clause here is the first time either party
sees a term, the contract is being used to smuggle something, and that is exactly what makes
people stop reading contracts.

---

## Part 1 — What the contract must contain

### 1. Who

* **The brand** — business name, and the person signing for it
* **The creator** — name, handle
* **INFLX** — named as the platform the collaboration runs on, not as a party to the commercial
  deal between them

### 2. What was already agreed, before this campaign

Carried straight from the handshake, so she recognises it:

* **What she is paid** — a commission %, a fee, or both
* **Where she posts** — the channels agreed
* **Working style** — she comes to the store, they ship to her, or she works remotely
* **When it was agreed**, and that this contract is the campaign built on it

### 3. This campaign

* **Campaign name**, and the **set** she is in
* **The aim** — sales, content, or both, stated in words rather than a code
* **Dates** — when the campaign starts, and for a content set when the posting window closes
* **Her post-by date**

### 4. What she may sell — sales and both

* **The whole store**, **a category**, or **named products**
* Stated as she would read it: *"You may sell anything in Women › Ethnic › Sarees."*
* ⚠️ Live scopes must say so: *"New products added to this category are included
  automatically."* A brand adding stock later changes what she can earn on, and she should
  know that at signing rather than discover it.

### 5. What her shoppers get — if anything

* The offer, in words: *"20% off sarees, until 30 September."*
* Where it can be used — in the shop, online, or both
* **If there is no offer, the contract says so plainly**: *"No shopper offer applies. Your
  followers buy at the brand's normal prices, and you earn your commission on those sales."*

### 6. What she is producing — content and both

* Each deliverable, counted: *"2 Instagram Reels, 3 Stories, 1 Post."*
* **The revision limit**, in plain terms: *"The brand may ask for changes twice. After that the
  work is accepted as delivered."*
* ⚠️ **No shot list, and no creative direction.** The brand states the aim and the products;
  what she makes is hers to decide. This is the founder's standing rule and the contract must
  not quietly reintroduce a brief.

### 7. The money

* **Commission** — the percentage, and on what: *"18% of the sale value, excluding delivery and
  taxes"*
* **Fee** — the amount, and when each part arrives:
  * **25%** when both parties sign
  * **25%** when the brand confirms the content is live
  * **50%** thirty days after that
* **The return window** — commission is held for **30 days** and released once returns can no
  longer happen
* **TDS at 10%**, deducted at withdrawal, not here
* **Minimum withdrawal ₹100**
* ⚠️ **The platform fee is the brand's, not hers.** The contract should say so explicitly, or
  she will assume it comes out of her share.

### 8. The shoot

* Which of the three: she visits, they ship, or remote
* For a visit — the address, the contact, and the agreed day
* For a shipment — that the goods are sent to her, that she confirms what arrived item by item,
  and that **anything missing is the brand's problem to fix, not hers to chase**
* That the goods remain the brand's property unless the contract says otherwise ⚠️ **founder
  to confirm — see Part 3**

### 9. Content usage — **NEW, and the founder's specific ask**

> *"content produced by creator can be used by inflx platform and related brand in their social
> media accounts but no exclusive rights on content but just usage only."*

Proposed wording:

> **You keep your work.**
>
> The content you make for this collaboration remains yours. You own it, and you may post,
> reuse, repost and archive it wherever and whenever you like.
>
> **You are giving permission to use it, not giving it away.**
>
> By signing, you allow **the brand** and **INFLX** to show this content on their own social
> media accounts and marketing channels — reposting it, featuring it, including it in a
> showcase of the campaign — with credit to you.
>
> **This permission is:**
>
> * **Non-exclusive** — you may still use the same content anywhere, including with other
>   brands, unless you have separately agreed otherwise
> * **Usage only** — neither the brand nor INFLX gains ownership, copyright, or any right to
>   sell or license your content to anyone else
> * **Unedited in substance** — they may crop or resize it to fit a format, but not alter its
>   meaning or remove your credit
>
> **You are not giving permission for:** paid advertising, print, packaging, billboards, or any
> use outside their own social and marketing channels. Those need a separate agreement and are
> usually paid separately.

⚠️ **Two questions in Part 3 turn on this clause. Please read them.**

### 10. If it stops

* Either party may end the collaboration before she has signed, at no cost
* After signing, what happens to money already released
* What happens if she does not deliver by the post-by date
* What happens if the brand ends the campaign early

### 11. Signatures

* Her name, typed, and the date
* The brand's, and the date
* **Both signatures activate it.** Tranche 1 releases then, and not before

## Part 2 — One structural point worth deciding now

Today the contract row stores only a few fields — the fee, the revision limit, the post-by date,
some names — and the screen composes the rest live from the campaign, the set and her
assignment.

**That means editing a set could change what a signed contract appears to say.**

A contract should be a **photograph, not a window.** At the moment she signs, every term should
be copied onto the contract and frozen. If the brand later changes the offer, extends the
campaign or reallocates products, the signed contract keeps saying what she agreed to, and the
change applies going forward.

⚠️ This is not a wording question, it is a data one, and it is the single most important thing
in this document. **A contract that can change after signing is not a contract.**

## Part 3 — What I need from you before this is real

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **How long does the usage permission last?** Forever, or a set period (say 2 years)? | Creators increasingly push back on perpetual usage. A term makes the deal easier to say yes to |
| **2** | **Does the permission survive the collaboration ending?** If she walks away mid-campaign, may the brand still use what she already made? | Ambiguity here is where disputes come from |
| **3** | **Who owns the goods after a shoot?** She keeps them, or sends them back? | The platform has no rule at all today |
| **4** | **Is there a non-compete?** May she work with a rival brand during the campaign? | Absent entirely right now |
| **5** | **What happens if she does not deliver?** | No rule exists |
| **6** | **Should a lawyer read this before it goes in front of real creators?** | ⚠️ My strong recommendation: yes. I can write clearly; I cannot tell you what is enforceable in India |

## Part 4 — What this needs in the software

Roughly, once the wording is agreed:

* Snapshot every term onto the contract at signing — **Part 2**, the biggest piece
* Store the usage-rights clause, and its duration, on the contract
* Show her the full picture **before** she signs: the agreement, the campaign, the allocation,
  the offer, the deliverables, the money, the usage clause
* Add the clause to the PDF

**None of that is worth starting until the wording is settled.**
