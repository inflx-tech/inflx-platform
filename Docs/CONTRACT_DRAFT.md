## What changed in v2, and why it moved

The founder split usage rights in two, and the split is the important part:

> *"usage rights means two things, usage for commercial, and just publishing content in social
> media accounts (in brand, in inflx social media accounts)... if brand want to use commercially
> then we keep it is agreed terms only — ask brand to tell 'I will use commercial purpose', for
> that if agree I will give some extra... this thing should settle even before detailed campaign
> creation (in agreement itself)."*

So there are **two different rights**, settled in **two different places**:

| | Where it is settled | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Showing the content on their own social accounts** | The contract. Included by default | No |
| **Using it commercially** | ⚠️ **The pre-campaign agreement**, negotiated | **Yes — extra** |

⚠️ **This is not a contract change. It is an agreement change.** A brand who wants commercial
rights has to say so **before** the campaign is ever built, when she can still say no, or say
yes for a price. Putting it in the contract would mean asking her at the moment she is trying to
start work — which is asking at the worst possible time, and she would feel it.

## Part 1 — What the contract contains

### 1. Who
* **The brand** — business name, and the person signing
* **The creator** — name, handle
* **INFLX** — named as the platform, not as a party to their commercial deal

### 2. What was already agreed, before this campaign
* **What she is paid** — a commission %, a fee, or both
* **Where she posts** — the agreed channels
* **Working style** — store visit, they ship, or remote
* **Whether commercial usage was agreed**, and what extra was agreed for it ⚠️ **new**
* **When it was agreed**

### 3. This campaign
* Campaign name, and the set she is in
* The aim, in words — sales, content, or both
* Dates, and for a content set the posting window
* Her post-by date

### 4. What she may sell — sales and both
* The whole store, a category, or named products, written as she would read it
* ⚠️ A live scope says so: *"New products added to this category are included automatically."*

### 5. What her shoppers get
* The offer in words, and where it can be used
* **If there is none, it says so**: *"No shopper offer applies. Your followers buy at the
  brand's normal prices, and you earn your commission on those sales."*

### 6. What she is producing — content and both
* Each deliverable, counted
* The revision limit in plain terms: *"The brand may ask for changes twice."*
* ⚠️ **No shot list, no creative direction.** The founder's standing rule; the contract must not
  quietly reintroduce a brief.

### 7. The money
* **Commission** — the percentage and what it applies to
* **Fee** — the amount, and the three tranches: **25%** on both signatures, **25%** when the
  brand confirms it is live, **50%** thirty days later
* **Commercial-usage extra**, if it was agreed ⚠️ **new** — stated as its own line so she can
  see what that permission earned her
* **Return window** — commission held **30 days**
* **TDS 10%** at withdrawal · **minimum withdrawal ₹100**
* ⚠️ **The platform fee is the brand's, not hers** — stated explicitly, or she will assume it
  comes out of her share

### 8. The shoot
* Which of the three, with the address and day for a visit
* For a shipment: goods are sent, she confirms item by item, and **anything missing is the
  brand's problem to fix, not hers to chase**
* *Who keeps the goods afterwards — **deferred**, founder 2026-08-28: "not required now, we
  discuss later." The contract stays silent rather than inventing a rule.*

### 9. Content usage — **the clause he asked for**

> **You keep your work.**
>
> The content you make for this collaboration remains yours. You own it, and you may post,
> reuse, repost and archive it wherever you like, including with other brands.
>
> **What you are agreeing to here — showing it.**
>
> The brand and INFLX may show this content on their **own social media accounts** — reposting
> it, featuring it, including it in a showcase of the campaign — with credit to you.
>
> This permission is **non-exclusive**, it is **showing only**, and neither the brand nor INFLX
> gains ownership, copyright, or any right to sell or license your work to anyone else. They may
> crop or resize it to fit a format, but not change its meaning or remove your credit.
>
> **This permission does not end when the collaboration does.** Content already published stays
> published. *(Founder, 2026-08-28.)*
>
> **What this does NOT include — commercial use.**
>
> Paid advertising, print, packaging, billboards, or any use beyond their own social accounts is
> **commercial use**, and it is **not** covered here.
>
> A brand who wants it must ask **before the campaign is built**, when terms are being agreed —
> and pay extra for it. If you agreed to that, it appears in the money section above, with the
> amount.

### 10. If it stops
* Either party may end it **before she signs**, at no cost
* After signing: what happens to money already released
* ⚠️ **If she does not deliver** — *"the brand may take legal action."* Founder, 2026-08-28:
  *"brand can initiate legal action (just we mention)."* Stated, not elaborated
* What happens if the brand ends the campaign early

### 11. Signatures
* Her name, typed, and the date
* The brand's, and the date
* **Both signatures activate it.** Tranche 1 releases then, and not before

## Part 2 — The structural point, and how his answer solves it

I raised this in v1: the contract stores only a few fields and the screen builds the rest live,
so **editing a set could change what a signed contract appears to say.**

His answer to Part 4 settles it:

> *"just show all details in pdf and he signs digitally simply"*

**A PDF generated at signing, carrying every term, IS the frozen photograph.** Once it exists it
cannot be edited by anything happening in the campaign afterwards. The screen can go on
composing live — the PDF is the record.

So: **generate the PDF at signing, with everything on it.** That is both what he asked for and
the fix for the problem, and it is simpler than snapshotting thirty columns.

## Part 4 — What this needs built

**In the agreement, before any campaign — the bigger half**

* A new optional term: **commercial usage**, with the extra amount
* It slots into the existing terms model beside `scope`: stated or not, marked requirement or
  preference, and **counterable** — she can say *"yes, for ₹8,000"*
* Shown on the request, in the counter, and on the agreement summary

**In the contract**

* Carry the usage clause, and the commercial term if it was agreed
* Show her the **whole picture before she signs**: the agreement, the campaign, what she may
  sell, the offer, the deliverables, the money, the usage clause
* Add the legal-action line

**In the PDF**

* Every term above, generated at signing
* Digital signature — typed name and timestamp, as it already works

**None of it is worth starting until question 4 is answered and the wording above is agreed.**
