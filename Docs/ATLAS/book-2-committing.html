Action Book 2 · Committing

Turning an agreement into a contract. 7 actions — 5 the brand starts, 2 the creator. Only one of them is new, and it is the one that replaces an entire screen the founder used to fill in twice.
Screens are referenced by address, never redrawn.

NEW does not exist · CHANGE exists and changes · KEEP works as it is · BOTH two people, so both phones are shown at the moment it matters
Why this phase used to be two phases. Before set-first there was an agreement, and then a separate second brief that asked for commission, deliverables, allocation, offer and channels all over again. BR-10 is that entire second brief collapsed into one screen, filled in once, at the moment he already has her in a set. Everything else in this book is machinery that already works.
BR-10Set one creator's termsNEW
StartsStep 4 of building a set
WhoBrand, alone
ScreensSB-29SB-30SB-34SB-35
Steps
  1. Defaults first, exceptions second. SB-29 One card sets the terms for everyone in the set. Below it, every person, each marked SAME or CHANGED.
    Eight creators, one screen — not eight forms. This is the difference between a set being useful and being eight times the work.
  2. Tapping anyone opens her own terms. SB-30
  3. What she earns. A commission %, a fee, or both — decided by the set's aim, not asked again. Her stated minimum shows as a hint: "she has said she will not go below 12%".
  4. What she can sell. Three widths:
    EVERYTHING — the brand's whole catalogue.
    A CATEGORY — SB-34 the live three-level tree. The path is stored, so products added later are included automatically.
    SOME PRODUCTS — SB-35 named individually. Out-of-stock ones are visible but not selectable.
  5. Her offer to shoppers → BR-11.
  6. Where she posts. Her agreed channels — and this list is what Book 4 checks her against when she goes live.
  7. What she makes. Deliverables. Optional for a sale set, required for a content set.
  8. How he may use it. The usage line she is agreeing to, in plain words.
Leaves behind
campaign_influencers — commission_pct / fee, alloc_scope + alloc_category_node_id / alloc_product_ids, brand_offer_id, channels[], formats_json.
agreements.terms_json → 'usage' — the usage text.
Can go wrong
The allocation must be readable as words, not as three columns. alloc_scope, alloc_category and alloc_product_ids mean nothing on a contract screen. One function turns them into "Men › Topwear › Jacket" — describeAllocation() — and both apps call it.

Deliverables asked for on a sale-only set. The wizard sends an empty list and the server calls that an error. The answer is one function, buysContent(goal), consulted by both sides — not two places deciding separately.

Steps
  1. SB-31 Three ways to answer:
    FROM THE LIBRARY — SB-36 built once, reused. Editing one that five live creators use warns first.
    BUILD A NEW ONE — SB-32 % off, ₹ off, or buy-X-get-Y, with a minimum cart and an end date.

An offer is what the shopper gets. Her commission is separate. They are worked out in that order — the discount first, her share of what is left second. The screen says so, because reading them as one number is the obvious mistake.
Leaves behind
brand_offers — the reusable definition.
campaign_influencers.brand_offer_id + offer_valid_from / until.
Can go wrong
An offer that expires before the go-live day. Nothing checks it today. An offer ending 10 Sep on a set going live 12 Sep is a discount nobody will ever get, and the first person to notice will be a shopper.

Can go wrong
The wallet check exists but the message does not. Today a short wallet produces a generic failure with no amount and no way to fix it there. The number and the button are the whole point of this screen.

Publishing set by set. Two sets with different aims have different money needs, and he must be able to send one without the other. Publishing the campaign as one indivisible thing forces him to fund a content set before he can start a sale set that needs nothing.

Can go wrong
Fixed 30 Aug — the reels error. Counter-signing a sale-only contract threw an error about reels. The deliverables check ran for every aim, so a contract that never bought content was rejected for not specifying any. It now runs only when content is actually being bought.

The founder reported it in one sentence: "content related reels and all is come mandatory only in pay for content type of campaign". He was right, and it was the same two-places-deciding fault as BR-02.
A remote shoot that says it is finished before it starts. Creating the shoot used to write status='completed' the moment the mode was remote. Fixed 30 Aug and six live shoots repaired — but this action is where that row is written, so it is the place it would come back.

Escrow has never actually run. escrow_entries, wallet_transactions and influencer_withdrawals are all at zero rows in production. The first fee contract to be counter-signed will be the first time tranche 1 has ever executed.
CR-07Decline a contractKEEPBOTH
StartsShe changes her mind at the contract
WhoCreator → brand
ScreensSC-08
Steps
  1. A reason from five, or her own words: not_relevant · fee_too_low · schedule_conflict · already_partnered · other.
  2. It ends. She leaves the set; the set carries on with everyone else.
Leaves behind
campaign_influencers.status → declined, with the reason and her note.
escrow_entries — her locked fee is released back to spendable.
Can go wrong
The list the app shows and the list the server accepts must be one list. Already shipped once as a bug: 'not_a_fit' was offered and was never valid. Now one shared constant, used by both apps.

Her fee staying locked after she declines. If escrow does not release, his wallet quietly holds money for a contract that no longer exists — and he cannot publish his next set. Nothing has ever exercised this path.

And the one this book carries unfixed: cancelInfluencer writes status='cancelled' to contract_status, which has no such value. Confirmed live. It will throw the first time anyone cancels rather than declines.
What this book leaves for the next one
Every path out of Phase 2
  • Both signed, store visit — the brand's move. He arranges a time → Book 3.
  • Both signed, remote — her move. She sends her address → Book 3.
  • Both signed, no shoot at all — a sale set where she uses her own material. She goes straight to building a shop post → Book 4.
  • She declined — out of the set, fee released, reason recorded.
What this book says must be true before Book 3 can work
  1. The shoot mode on the contract has to be right, and it has to be the set's. Everything in Book 3 forks on it, and the mode is resolved here, at counter-signing.
  2. Escrow has to actually move. Tranche 1 fires at BR-14 and has never fired. If it silently does nothing, Book 5 has nothing to pay out and nobody finds out for a month.
  3. The contract and the invitation must render from the same fields. Otherwise she signs something she did not read, and no test would catch it.
The pattern this book is the fifth instance of
Two things doing one job. The wizard and the server both deciding whether content is bought (BR-02). The deliverables check and the aim both deciding whether reels are required (BR-14). The app's decline list and the server's decline list (CR-07). Each one shipped as a bug, and each one is fixed the same way — one function, called by both sides.
