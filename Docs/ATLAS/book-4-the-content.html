Action Book 4 · The content

Shop posts on INFLX, then everyone goes live together. 7 actions — 4 the creator, 2 the brand, 1 admin. Two are new, and both are places where somebody is currently told nothing.

NEW · CHANGE · KEEP · BOTH both phones shown · SHOP POST on INFLX · PAID CONTENT the brand bought · ADMIN
Two different things are called "content" and they are reviewed by different people. A shop post lives on INFLX, sells products, and is reviewed by admin. A deliverable is a reel or story the brand paid for, and is reviewed by the brand. They have different deadlines, different reviewers, and different consequences — and confusing them is the easiest mistake in this phase.

StartsShe has material and a collab that sells
WhoCreator, alone
ScreensSA-01SC-21SC-22SC-23SC-24SC-25SC-27SC-28
Steps
  1. SC-21 The Create tab lists the collabs that need a shop post, each with its go-live day.
  2. 1 of 3 — who is it for. SC-22 Scope first, products second.
    ONE BRAND — then which campaign, and she may use anything that campaign allows.
    A FEW BRANDS — every campaign she has with those brands, pooled into one post.
    EVERYTHING — every product from every collab she is in.
  3. 2 of 3 — which. SC-23 The campaigns, and the count of products each one opens up.
  4. 3 of 3 — build it. SC-24 Photos, tag products from her allowed list, caption. A readiness card names what is still missing.
  5. Send for review. SC-25 It goes to admin, not to the brand — and the screen says so.
The scope decides one thing only: which products she is allowed to put in. Nothing else about the post changes. The founder's rule, and it is what makes all three scopes the same piece of code — the backend resolves brand → campaign → what she was allocated, and the three scopes are three widths of the same query.
Leaves behind
creator_posts — scope, the campaigns it draws from, tagged products, photos, caption, status='in_review'.
Prices and offers are read from the live product, never typed.
Can go wrong
Asking for products before establishing scope. That is the current order, and it makes the product list either wrong or enormous. Scope first is the whole change.

creator_posts has no campaign_influencer_id. A post cannot say which collaboration it belongs to. This one missing column blocks five features — including counting shop posts per collab (AD-03) and attributing a sale back to a contract.

A price typed rather than read. The most common reason a post is sent back. If the composer ever stores a price instead of a product reference, they drift the moment the brand changes the price.
AD-01Review a shop postCHANGEBOTHADMIN
StartsA post is submitted
WhoAdmin → creator
ScreensSA-01SA-02SC-25SC-26
Steps
  1. SA-01 The queue, ordered by the nearest go-live day — not by when it was submitted.
  2. SA-02 Three things are checked by the machine, and shown as ticks and crosses:
    • every product is hers to sell, under her allocation
    • every price matches the live product
    • the offer is still running on the go-live day
  3. A human judges only the photos and the caption.
  4. Two answers:
    APPROVE — it can go live.
    SEND IT BACK — with a reason she will read word for word. → CR-13.
The machine checks what a machine can check. Nobody should be comparing a price in a caption against a product page by eye, forty times a day. The founder's own example — "the price does not match" — is exactly the check that should never have needed a person.
Leaves behind
creator_posts.status → approved / sent_back, with the reason and the failing product.
Can go wrong
A queue ordered by submission time. A post approved after its go-live day is worthless. Ordering by urgency is not a nicety — it is the difference between a campaign working and not.

A rejection reason written for admin, not for her. She has to be able to fix it from what it says. "Validation failed" is not a reason.

StartsThe go-live day arrives
WhoCreator → brand
ScreensSC-16SB-50
Steps
  1. SC-16 One card per channel she agreed to — matched to the list on her contract, not a single Instagram box.
  2. She posts on her own channels, then pastes each link.
  3. The two aims want completely different things here:
    PAY FOR CONTENT — every agreed channel is required. Two channels means two links, and the button stays dark at 1 of 2. Completing them releases the second quarter of her fee.
    PAY PER SALE — one link is enough, and it is only a reference. The money comes from the shop post, not from the link.
The founder's correction, and it changed the design. Not "live on Instagram" — live on social media, per channel, matched to her agreed list. A creator on Instagram and YouTube has two lines to fill and the brand has two to confirm.

Leaves behind
A confirmation per channel.
escrow_entries — tranche 2 released, only when all agreed channels are confirmed.
Can go wrong
Releasing money on the first confirmed channel. Two channels agreed, one confirmed, and half her obligation is unmet while a quarter of the fee has moved.

A brand who never confirms. Her money sits behind a button he has no deadline to press. This needs an automatic release after a stated number of days, or it becomes a way to withhold payment by doing nothing. Not designed yet — flagged here because this is where it bites.
What this book leaves for the next one
Every path out of Phase 4
  • Shop post approved and live — it is selling. Every sale starts the 7-day clock → Book 5.
  • Every channel confirmed on a content set — tranche 2 released, tranche 3 waiting → Book 5.
  • Sent back — she fixes it, and the go-live day has not moved.
  • Two revisions used and still not right — he approves it or ends the collaboration.
The two schema decisions this book forces
  1. creator_posts needs campaign_influencer_id. Without it a post cannot name its collaboration, and five features stay blocked — including counting posts per collab and tracing a sale back to a contract.
  2. Live links must be a row per channel, not a column. One post_url makes the founder's per-channel correction unbuildable.
The unanswered question
Nothing forces a brand to confirm she went live. Tranche 2 sits behind BR-19, and BR-19 has no deadline. A brand who simply never presses the button withholds 25% of her fee indefinitely, without ever refusing. An automatic release after a set number of days is the obvious answer and it has not been designed. It belongs in the same admin settings screen as the other three waits — SA-04.
