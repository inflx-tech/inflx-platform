Decision 1 — working style: three into two

Dropping "we ship to her" would orphan the whole parcel flow — the itemised contents, the item-by-item receipt, the missing-item flag. That was a real piece of the shoot design.
Why three felt confusing: they answer two different questions. Store visit and remote answer where does she shoot. Shipping answers how do the products reach her. Putting them in one list forces a choice between things that aren't alternatives.
Asked at the agreementThen
Store visit — she comes to youSlot, address, contact. No parcel.
Remote — she shoots at her endOne follow-up: are you sending products? Yes → the parcel flow. No → nothing to ship.
Two choices in the negotiation, exactly as you wanted. The parcel survives as a consequence of remote, not a third confusing peer.

Decision 2 — the follower split you asked for is half-buildable

NumberReality
Followers on our platformreal — a follow table exists and is being written to
Instagram followersreal — scraped daily
YouTube subscribersdoes not exist — no count is stored anywhere, and not one creator has a YouTube handle on file
So the block is drawn with YouTube as an unconnected slot rather than a fabricated number. The moment a creator connects a channel and it's scraped, the row fills in. Showing "0" would read as "she has no audience there", which isn't what we know.

Why groups
A group is a list you keep. Ask them once, then negotiate with each one separately — the group sends the message, it doesn't merge the deals.
Groups. The table exists and has never held a row — this is its first screen.
The tab has a hard boundary. A conversation lives in Creators from the first approach until a campaign is drafted from it. The moment it's briefed, it moves to Campaigns and never comes back. No item is ever in both.

Pitches and requests share one list on purpose. Both are "a conversation with a creator about terms". Splitting them by who started it would make a brand check two places for the same job.
A broadcast is one row, not thirty. It expands into its thirty conversations, each negotiated separately — which is what the engine already does.

How they'll drive traffic

InstagramYouTubeWhatsAppTheir INFLX shop
🔒 On a content set this step asks the opposite — how many reels and stories you're buying. Here you're not buying posts, so we don't dictate them.
The fundamental difference, on screen. Sales asks what reach they bring; content specifies what you're buying.
Why this reframing matters. On a content set the brand is buying labour and can reasonably say "1 reel + 2 stories". On a sales set the creator carries the performance-marketing burden and earns only if they sell — so dictating post counts is both unfair and pointless. Asking about their channels instead tells you what reach you're getting without pretending to own their output.

The boundary

You seeYou never see
Sales through a creator, on your campaignsHer sales for any other brand
Your products' performanceAnother brand's products in a mixed post

Mixed posts are the sharp edge. A post carrying you and another label is invisible to both of you, because showing it to one reveals she works with the other. Analytics must not quietly break that.
She sees the mirror. What she earned, from which brand, through which post. Same data, cut by who's asking.

Build order

#SliceSizeNote
1Five-tab nav + Home reworksmallEvery source exists. Alerts moves under Profile.
2Creators tab — Talking, Agreed, Find, GroupsmediumMostly rehoming existing screens into one hub
3Follower split on every creator rowsmallPlatform count is a new query; YouTube is a slot
4Working style → two questions, everywheresmallComposer, agreement, and off the contract screen
5Campaign creation on mobile — 4 stepslargeThe wizard, the reframed basics, agreements as the source
6The searchable place pickermediumNeeds the place list. Reused by every filter.
7Campaigns → six steps + history per stepmediumShares the diary with the creator app
8Products by category · exclusive posts · analytics cutsmediumSame groupings the creator side needs
Slices 1–4 are worth doing first and together. They're small, they need no new data model, and they're what make the app feel like it matches how you now describe the business. Slice 5 is the big one and depends on nothing above it — it can run in parallel.
Next: brand web. It mirrors this exactly, plus the catalogue. Since campaign creation is now on both, web's version is the same four steps with more room — not a different design.
