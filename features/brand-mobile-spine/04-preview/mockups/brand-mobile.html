The rule that decides every screen in this file. If a step is a negotiation, brand mobile gets the full thing — asking, countering, accepting. If a step is the other side's job, brand mobile shows its state and offers nothing to press. If it is web only, brand mobile says so plainly and hands over, rather than half-doing it.
Only two jobs stay on the desk — listing products, and building the detailed campaign. Everything else a brand does, they can do from the phone. That matches the scope you locked earlier and this mockup does not widen it.

One agreement. "See how you got here" opens the step-1 diary — the same page she sees.
Half-doing this on mobile would be worse than not doing it. A campaign needs sets, aims, posting windows, what each creator may sell, and offers — against a catalogue. A cut-down phone version would produce campaigns that then need fixing on the desk anyway.
What mobile genuinely adds: the expiry warning. An agreement lapses at 90 days and the brand loses the whole negotiation. Surfacing "expires in 9 days" on the phone is worth more than a phone campaign builder.
The web screen already accepts this handoff — the campaign wizard pre-fills from an agreement and warns when the brand drifts from what was agreed. Mobile only has to point at it.

The boundary, precisely

The brand seesThe brand never sees
Sales through this creator, on their campaignsHer sales for any other brand
Their own products' performanceAnother brand's products in a mixed post
Reel numbers for content they paid forHer posts that don't involve them
Her public followers and engagementHer total earnings across the platform
Posts using only their productsMixed-brand posts — nobody is told
Their own campaign totalsAny other brand's campaigns or rates
Mixed posts are the sharp edge. Asha's "Wedding Edit" carries two brands. Neither is notified and neither sees its numbers — because showing it to one reveals she works with the other. That rule already exists; analytics must not quietly break it.
She sees the mirror image — what she earned, from which brand, through which post. Same data, cut by who is asking.
What brand mobile must not do
You asked for both halves. This is the half that keeps the app honest.

Two of these are new restrictions, not restatements. The brand can currently set the working style at contract approval with no say from the creator — that moves into the negotiation. And "Add to campaign" on a creator profile becomes "Request a collaboration", because approaching someone with a campaign already built is the thing this whole programme inverted.

Slices 1 and 2 are worth doing alone. Neither needs the shoot, the parcel, the ordering rule, or any decision still open — and they are the two that make the phone feel like it knows what is going on.
Next: brand web. It inherits the same six steps and the same ownership table, plus the two jobs that live only there — building the campaign from agreements, and the catalogue. The spine drawn here is what web will mirror, not the other way round.
