Twelve screens, four steps, one new idea
Everything here comes out of FINAL 2. Nothing was redesigned. What changed is which of those screens Stage 2 actually builds, and what each one costs against the code that exists today.
The one new idea, and the reason this fits on a phone at all. The old wizard asked the brand to invent commission, dates and terms for every creator. After the handshake all of that already exists — so step 4 section ① is a read-back, and the only genuine decision left in the whole wizard is one offer.

Two things already on this phone get reused rather than rebuilt. The offer builder — the offers library already creates discounts on the phone — and the category picker, already backed by the real listing tree. Neither is redrawn here.
One thing gets merged, not added. The brand app already carries two product pickers — a full screen for the request composer, and a modal for the offer builders. Ledger #86 says this picker has four callers. A third would guarantee they drift, which is the fault behind four of Stage 1's twenty-one bugs. So the first slice of this stage merges them.
What is deliberately not here. Open-group sets, cold invites, adding or pricing products, and the needs-you drill-down. The first two are removed by ledger #71 and #76; the third is the founder's boundary; the fourth is Stage 3.

Why the shell is so thin. Everything that used to sit at campaign level — budget, end date, who posts — either belongs to a set or was already settled at the agreement. What is left is a name and a start date.
The server already agrees with this. POST /campaigns takes a name and a start date and ignores any budget or end date sent to it — it writes NULL for both and derives them from the sets. That rule shipped in Part 5. This screen is the first one built to match it.
A set is the unit of work. One aim, one group of creators, one scope of products, one offer. Two sets in a campaign are two independent arrangements that happen to share a name.

Why "channels" replaces "content format" on sales. On content the brand buys labour and can reasonably say "1 reel + 2 stories". On sales the creator only earns if she sells — dictating post counts is unfair and unenforceable. Asking about her channels tells you the reach you are getting without pretending to own her output.
Channels do not exist anywhere today. I searched the whole backend and all 156 migrations: the only occurrence of the word is a comment. This is the stage's one new database column — additive, nullable, guidance only, blocking nothing.
Disabled-and-explained beats hidden. A brand who has used the old wizard will look for the budget field. Showing it greyed with "you pay nothing up front" teaches the model; removing it silently just looks broken.

The rules on this step

Rule
74Filtered by aim. A sales set cannot show a content agreement — the terms would not mean the same thing. The count of what was hidden is shown, so nothing disappears silently
73One agreement, one set. Once used it leaves the pool, so it cannot be double-briefed

Everything this screen needs is already delivered. GET /brands/me/agreements returns the photo, the handle, the settled terms, the date agreed and the expiry — filtered to ready-and-unexpired. The aim is inside the terms. No new endpoint.
How #77 is honoured without touching a working transaction. The agreement is consumed inside invite, guarded so it can never be briefed twice. So the phone simply does not invite until publish — ticking is local state. Abandon the wizard and nothing was written at all.
The risk that creates, and how it is answered. An agreement can lapse between ticking and publishing. That is why "a chosen agreement expired mid-wizard" is one of the five publish blockers, and why it has to be built here rather than assumed.
Cold invites are the deliberate exception. If you ever need to invite someone with no agreement, that stays a separate flow with full accept, decline and negotiate on her side. It does not sneak in here, because everything downstream assumes terms are settled.

Two already exist and they are not the same thing. BrandProductPickerScreen is a full screen with search and a category filter; ProductPickerSheet is a 95-line modal with a flat list and no images. Building a third for allocation is exactly how the four end up disagreeing. Slice 1 of this stage merges them and re-points both callers.
The data is already there. GET /brands/me/products returns a resolved image_url, the price, and the category — and GET /brands/me/listing-tree returns the tree. Nothing new is needed on the server for this picker.
"Other" is deliberate. Uncategorised products stay visible and selectable, with a nudge to tag them. Hiding them would hide real stock — and right now every practice product is uncategorised, so without this group the picker would look empty and broken on the first walk.
Auto-join is the difference that matters. Store and category scopes pick up new products silently; hand-picked lists do not. A brand who hand-picks and then adds stock will wonder why she is not selling it — so each option says so before it is chosen.

The preview. Which offer wins, and what the creator earns.96 · 97
The preview is the piece worth insisting on. Offer stacking is the single most confusing thing about running a shop, and a brand publishing an offer they have never seen resolve is how you get "why did this sell for that price?" a week later.
It must use the same resolver the shopping app uses. resolveBestOffer already exists and already picks one winner, never stacking. This stage adds a brand-authenticated door onto it — not a second copy of the maths. A preview that can disagree with checkout is worse than no preview.
The offer builder is not redrawn. The brand app already creates discounts from the offers library, with the five types validated server-side. This stage points the wizard at what exists and adds only the scope-via-shared-picker step and the preview.
Section ② is closed on a content set, with the reason stated — nothing is being sold, so there is no basket for an offer to apply to. Payment stages (25 / 25 / 50) appear in its place, and the wallet requirement is named.

This is the largest backend gap in the stage, and the one that matters commercially. A creator can be published into a sales set today with nothing to sell — contracted, and invisible to every shopper. The wallet is the only thing publish currently refuses.
Drift is warned, never blocked. A brand may have a good reason to change a term. What must never happen is changing it silently — she is told which one moved, by name, when the invite lands. Two places where the agreed board and the live code disagree. Both are named in the spec with an answer, rather than resolved in silence.

One — when an agreement is consumed. The board says on publish. The code does it inside the invite. Answer: leave the transaction alone and never invite until publish, so ticking writes nothing and abandoning burns nothing.

Two — where a content set's budget comes from. The board says calculated. The code requires it typed. Answer: the server derives it from the sum of that set's agreed fees, exactly as it already derives the campaign total. Web keeps sending a typed value and is overwritten by the identical number.

Build order

#SliceWhy here
1Merge the two product pickers into one componentFour callers depend on it. Merge first, or build a third and guarantee the drift
2Server: derived set budget · the channels column · the four publish blockersEvery screen above validates against these. Building screens first means building them twice
3The app's API layer: create and update campaigns and setsFive thin methods. Nothing can be built until they exist
4Landing rework, shell, sets list, step 1, step 2 with its three branchesThe spine of the wizard, all against endpoints that already work
5Step 3 — the agreements pick-list and the agreement detailNeeds the aim filter and the expiry countdown; no new endpoint
6Step 4 — both sections, allocation, the offerHangs off slice 1's picker and the existing offer builder
7The basket preview endpoint and its screenA door onto the existing resolver
8Review, drift, the five blockers, publishLast, because it validates everything above it
The gate has not moved. This stage adds a deep new route tree to a native stack — the change class that passes every automatic check and then crashes on a handset, skipped six times running. The founder walks it on a phone before any deploy is requested, and the duplicate-route check runs across every stack in both apps.
Nothing here is outward-facing. No creator sees a change, no shopper sees a change, and nothing a brand can already do stops working. The only behaviour that reaches an existing surface is the derived set budget, which overwrites a typed value with the number web already displays.
