Eight endpoints, no screen, and an empty box
The shoot is the best-built step in the whole spine and the only one nobody can see. It is also the only step that already writes down everything that happens to it.
Already live, and untouched by this stage. A shoot plan is created the moment both sides sign. Propose a slot · accept · counter-propose · flip a store visit to remote · dispatch · confirm receipt · mark done · cancel. The legal date window is computed server-side — post-by minus two days, falling back to the posting window minus three. Two reschedules per side, counted separately, then locked. And every action writes to a log.
And there is no screen. Grep both navigators for a production route: zero hits. The shoot lives as a panel inside the contract page on both phones — BrandContractScreen:628 and ContractScreen:366. So a notification saying "Asha suggested Tuesday" can only point at a contract and hope she scrolls. Stage 3's step 3 currently says "gets its own screen in Stage 4".
The box has no contents. A shipment carries a courier, a tracking number and four timestamps, and no idea what is inside it. Searched the whole schema and backend: nothing. That is the genuinely new work here — and the picker it needs was built in Stage 2 and is waiting for its fourth caller.

Its fourth caller, exactly as predicted. Ledger #86 said this picker has four callers — allocation, offer scope, the parcel builder, and the creator tagging products in a post. Stage 2 merged two implementations into one component precisely so this stage would not build a third. Nothing new is designed here; it is configured.
Picked, not typed, is the whole point. If a brand types "3 sarees" into a note, she confirms a sentence and tags a product, and the two can never be reconciled. Picking from the catalogue makes the box, her receipt and her post the same three objects.
A parcel with nothing in it is refused. Dispatch needs at least one item. Today it would happily send an empty box, because there was no box to fill.
A resend is a second parcel, not an edit. It gets its own courier, its own tracking number and its own delivery. Overwriting the first would erase the fact that something went wrong.

Today this is one button and a free-text box. confirm-received stamps a timestamp and takes a note. She cannot say which item is missing, so the only route is a message to a human — which is exactly the chasing this screen removes.
And it does one more thing it should not. Confirming receipt currently sets the shoot to completed. So a courier handing over a box would finish her shoot — advancing her to step 4 before she has photographed anything, on both phones. This stage splits them: receiving moves it to in-progress; the shoot finishes when she says it is done.
"Mark one damaged" needs a decision. Missing and damaged are different problems and the platform has no rule about who bears a damaged item. The recommendation is to record it and treat it exactly like missing — she shoots what she can, the brand resends or drops it. That invents no liability rule. It is the founder's call.

The rule underneath this screen. A shortfall is the brand's problem. She is never asked to chase it, never blocked by it, and never has to explain it. The two buttons are the brand's two honest options and there is no third.
"Removes that saree from what she can sell" needs care. If she was allocated the whole store or a category, that item is still in scope — Part 4 defined those scopes as resolved live, and carving a hole in one would be inventing a rule. So dropping removes the id only on a hand-picked allocation. Stated here because it is the one place this board reaches into another stage's machinery.
Nothing about this is new machinery. A second parcel is another dispatch on the same shoot, which the existing endpoint already does. What is new is knowing which item it replaces.
