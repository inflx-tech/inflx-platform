THE ACTION ITSELF
The rule that makes the ladder work. A number at any level is the sum of everything beneath it, plus anything owed at that level itself. So a badge on Campaigns is never a mystery — tap it and it decomposes, every time, until you reach a button.
The calculation already exists, and it already reconciles. Stage 0 built collabCounts and it has been running on every inbox call since. Panel ⑦ shows it run against production, unchanged — the tree comes out correct at every level. This stage is overwhelmingly a screen job.
And the brand app throws it away. BrandHomeScreen and BrandActionRequiredScreen both fetch the endpoint that carries the tree. Grep the whole app for needs_you: zero hits. It is computed on every call and discarded.

LEVEL 1
The yellow number is everything owed inside that campaign. 3 + 2 = the 5 on the tab.
The tab-bar badge is the only number that spans both halves. 5 from Campaigns plus 3 from Creators = 8, which is exactly what the server returns for this brand today. The brand app currently shows no tab badge at all — turning it on is the one change a brand notices without opening anything, and it is the founder's open question in the spec.
Ended campaigns carry no badge, even if something technically remains. Nothing owed on a finished campaign should pull attention from live work. They stay in the list and still open all the way down — a finished campaign is a track record.
A campaign with nothing owed shows no badge at all — not a zero. A zero reads as a state; an absence reads as calm.

The offer-expiry row needs its correction stated. The roll-up carries a comment saying this cannot be computed because offers have no expiry. That was true on 15 Aug and is no longer: campaign_influencers.offer_valid_until and brand_offers.store_ends_at both exist. CREATOR
Her side. Same step numbers, mirrored turn.
What she sees today, and why it must be deleted rather than fixed. Her detail screen draws its own spine — Invite · Nego · Sign · Active · Track · Done — which is the OLD model and not the agreed six. Worse, it matches on stage names that Stage 1 renamed. The two lists overlap in exactly one value, so for almost every collaboration nothing is highlighted at all. Her spine is currently six grey labels.
A step with no button still shows its state. Hiding step 4 from the brand would make the collaboration look stalled. Greyed, with "not yours to review", tells them work is happening and it is not theirs.
Where Stage 4 and 5 have not arrived, the step opens its history and says which stage brings the rest. A tap that goes nowhere is worse than a tap that explains itself.

Every level reconciles, on real data, today. That is the single most important fact in this contract: the hard part of Stage 3 is already built and already right. What is missing is the screens that show it, and three campaign-level plus two set-level items that are not counted yet.
A badge never lies about depth. If Festive Season shows 3 and its two sets show 2 and 1, the parts are on the screen. If a level's parts cannot be found, the badge is wrong — numbers that do not reconcile are how people stop trusting them.
Six steps are represented in this one snapshot — 2, 3, 4 and 6, with three different turns. The practice world is varied enough to walk the whole ladder without inventing data.
One thing to know before the walk. Not one contract is signed — contract_signed_at and brand_signed_at are NULL on all six collaborations, including the ones marked "active". The step derivation reads the contract's own status rather than those columns, so the numbers above are right — but a brand seeing step 2 for someone marked active will reasonably ask why.
