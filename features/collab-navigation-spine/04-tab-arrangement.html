The shape in one line. The creator's app is one big tab and four small ones — 54 of her 67 actions are in Collabs, because a creator's whole working life is one collaboration at a time. The brand's app is two big tabs — Creators 33, Campaigns 57 — because a brand does two genuinely different jobs: finding people, and running work.
Neither app gains a tab. The creator keeps Home · Collabs · Create · Earnings · Profile. The brand swaps Alerts → Creators and keeps five. Alerts moves under Profile — a tab is for something you go to on purpose; a feed you check when a badge appears is not that.
Why the totals overshoot 160. 67 + 98 = 165, because 24 actions are "either side" and appear in both apps — the conversation, the diary, the shoot, completion. They are one screen built once and shipped twice, counted in each app's total.

The five rules that decided every placement
If a future screen doesn't fit these, the rule is wrong or the screen is.

The rules

RuleWhat it settled
1One action, one home tab. An action may be surfaced elsewhere but is owned once.Needs-you appears on Home but is owned by Collabs. Earnings mirrors the per-collaboration figure owned by Collabs.
2Before a campaign vs after. For the brand, Creators owns everything up to a draft; Campaigns owns everything after.A conversation moves out of Creators the moment it's briefed and never returns. No item is ever in both.
3Making vs owing. Create owns what she makes; Collabs owns what she owes.A post can span several brands, so it belongs to her. Step 4 of a collaboration links across rather than duplicating the composer.
4A tab is a destination, a feed is not.Alerts loses its tab to Creators and moves under Profile. The badge that mattered moved to Home, where the work is.
5Either-side actions are one screen, shipped twice.The conversation, the diary, the shoot and completion are built once and appear in both apps — same buttons, same words, names swapped.
