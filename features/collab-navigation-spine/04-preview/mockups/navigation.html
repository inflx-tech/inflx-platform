Collabs — the one spine, and what's needed from you

features/collab-navigation-spine · navigation mockup for lock · 2026-08-15

Six phases. One tab. One grammar.
Everything from the first approach to the finished collaboration is a single lifecycle. Today it is spread across four tabs and eleven screens, and nothing tells a creator where she is in it.

The design rule for everything that follows: a collaboration sits at exactly one phase, and that phase is both its place in the list and its position in the stepper. The tab you find it under and the step you are on are the same fact, shown twice. Nothing can be in two places.

The one real IA tension, stated up front. Shop posts are created in the Create tab — that is correct, because a post can span several brands or her whole shelf and belongs to her, not to one collaboration. But a post made from the Kanchi Silks shoot is also step 4 of the Kanchi Silks collaboration. Create owns the making; Collabs owns the obligation. Step 4 links across rather than duplicating the composer.
What this costs. Phases 01, 02 and 05 already have screens — they gain a spine around them. Phase 03 needs a screen. Phase 04 is a link plus a status roll-up. Phase 06 is new. The navigation work is mostly arrangement, which is why it is worth doing before the Part A/B/C build rather than after.

The six stages, and what decides them

StageA collaboration is here when
Invitationsbrand-initiated, no contract yet — request, counter, or agreement waiting to be briefed
Pitchescreator-initiated, no contract yet
To signa campaign invite exists and is unsigned
Shootcontract signed, shoot not marked done
Contentshoot done, not yet complete — covers steps 4 and 5
Donecompletion satisfied for its track
Why steps 4 and 5 share one stage. They overlap by design — the ordering rule holds publishing back while both reviews run concurrently. Splitting them into two stages would put one collaboration in two tabs at once, which is exactly the rule this IA is built on. The stepper inside shows the two clearly; the list does not need to.
Replaces, not adds. Today's stage list is All · Invites · Applied · Negotiating · To sign · Active · Tracking · Completed · Archive — nine, three of which (applied, negotiating, tracking) are derived states most creators never see, and applied can never be non-empty at all (Part 6 found nothing in the backend writes status='applied'). Six replaces nine.
The header pill "4 need you" is the entry to panel ⑤. The tab-bar badge is the same number — the one thing worth interrupting her for.
Inside one collaboration — the spine is the navigation
Not a dashboard. A checklist that knows where you are, what you did, and what is holding the next thing up. Every step is a door to a screen already designed.

No new screens are invented here. Every step opens something already designed — the invitation thread, the contract, the shoot screen, the post list, the go-live screen, the completion screen. The spine is the index.
A locked step must say its condition. "Opens once one shop post is live — so people who tap through land on something" does the work a disabled button never does: it explains, so she is not left guessing whether the app is broken.

Steps 4 and 5 are the whole difference. On sales, we review for quality and the brand never sees a post before it is live. On content, the brand reviews because they are paying for it. Same position in the spine, opposite reviewer — the badge on the step says which, every time.
Money shows only where money exists. The sales spine has no payment line at any step: no wallet, no escrow, no stages. She earns when someone buys. The content spine names the amount at steps 2, 5 and 6, because those releases are real.
Step 3 is identical on both. The shoot does not care what it is for — which is why it deserves one screen and one flow rather than being reimplemented per track.

Two changes to the screen itself. The five tabs become one list grouped by urgency — five tabs means five places to check, and the brand's real question is "what is on fire", not "what kind of thing is on fire". And it moves from brandPalette yellow to ig, matching everything else.
Kill the Applications tab. It renders a counter that is structurally always zero — the flow behind it was never finished, as Part 6 recorded. A permanently empty tab teaches people to stop looking at the screen.
One summary endpoint, extended. getSummary is already a single round-trip counts query. The new sources are added there rather than in five more calls.
The brand app has no Collabs tab — and needs the same spine
Brand tabs today: Home · Campaigns · Wallet · Alerts · Profile. The per-creator lifecycle lives three levels down, under Campaigns → Campaign → Creators.
The asymmetry. The creator sees one collaboration with one brand. The brand sees one campaign with many creators. That is a real difference, not a bug — so the answer is not to bolt a Collabs tab onto the brand app. The brand's spine is per-creator inside a campaign, plus one cross-campaign action list.

No new brand tab. Every screen above is an existing route gaining the spine, plus one new detail screen. Campaign creation and listing stay web-only, per locked scope — nothing here needs a desk.
Alerts keeps the badge, Needs-you keeps the work. Alerts is a feed of what happened; Needs-you is a list of what is owed. Today the brand has both and they overlap confusingly — the split is: if it needs a decision it belongs in Needs you, if it is news it belongs in Alerts.

Native-crash warning, stated because it has bitten this repo before. This adds several routes to native stacks in both apps. Two Stack.Screens with the same name inside one stack is a hard crash on tab mount that no ErrorBoundary catches. Checked: there are none today in either navigator. The same name in different stacks is fine and is already the pattern — CampaignDetail sits in both HomeStack and CampaignsStack, ContractPdf in three. That is legitimate; the thing to preserve is uniqueness within each stack as these routes are added, and to prove it on a device rather than in a bundle build.

Build order

#SliceWhy here
1Stage model 9 → 6 + step derivation, server-sideEverything else reads the step number. One source, two apps.
2CollabDetail becomes the spineThe index. Existing steps wire in immediately; new ones slot in as they land.
3Creator Needs you + header pill + tab badgeStandalone value from day one, even before Parts A–C
4Brand Needs you rework + campaign step numbersSame data, other side
5Brand per-creator spineMirrors slice 2
This is arrangement, and it comes first. Slices 1–5 need none of Part A, B or C — they organise what exists and leave labelled sockets for what does not. Build the spine, then drop the shoot, the parcel, the ordering rule and completion into steps that are already there.
