The collaboration screens, redrawn around one idea: what can be negotiated depends on how she is being paid. Every screen here is drawn from the live app.

One rule underneath all ten changes

Paying for content and paying for sales are different deals, so different things are on the table. Today the app treats them almost identically, and that is what produces the ₹0 and the wrong questions.

Why deliverables belong only to a fee

A fee buys a specific piece of work, so both sides must know what it buys. A commission buys nothing — she earns only if she sells, so counting her posts is measuring the wrong thing. That single distinction fixes two of your ten points at once: the brand is now asked for deliverables on a fee deal, and the creator stops being asked for them on a sales deal.

"Where she works" can no longer be a requirement

Today you mark it requirement — meaning it cannot be countered — or preference. You have asked for it to be negotiable, and a term cannot be both. So it moves up beside money and time, and the requirement/preference marker survives only on what she promotes.

Nothing live is disturbed

Checked before drawing: all 14 requests and 8 agreements in production are pay-per-sale, none uses a posting window, and none states deliverables. Every change here lands on empty ground.

Deliberately reusing it rather than inventing a second list

This programme has twice had to clean up a vocabulary that got written two different ways — offer types on web versus mobile, and a duplicated offer-type normaliser. A third would cost more than it saves. These five words already travel from a campaign brief into a contract; a handshake that used different ones would need translating at every boundary.

If this list is wrong, now is the moment to change it — but it should change everywhere, not just here.

Counts, not free text. "2 reels and 3 stories" is checkable — the platform can tell whether she delivered. A sentence cannot be checked by anything except an argument later.

"✓ fine" is doing real work

You asked that if the date is acceptable she taps OK and the same date carries. That is what this is — but it also removes the commonest cause of a confusing counter: a creator who only wanted more money but re-typed the date slightly differently and moved two terms without meaning to.

And deliverables appear here only on a fee deal — the same stepper the brand used, pre-filled with what the brand asked for. On a sales deal the question is absent.

The listing tree, and what it reaches

This is the largest of the ten and the only one that leaves the collaboration screens. Drawing it separately so it can be judged — or deferred — on its own.

You asked for two connected things:

  • a category chosen from the listing engine tree, not typed
  • and that choice constraining which products can appear in her post
What exists, and what does not

The tree is real and the follower app already browses it. The brand phone app has never been connected to it — no category picker, no product picker, no endpoint feeding either. The product list does have a backend already, so "specific products" is much cheaper than "a category".

My recommendation: split it

Take specific products and the category picker with the other nine changes — they are part of the same screen and the same conversation.

Take "only those products appear in her post" separately. It is not a collaboration-screen change at all; it is a rule about posting, and it touches the post composer, the product picker and the scope check. Bolting it onto this batch would double the size and delay the nine changes that are ready.
